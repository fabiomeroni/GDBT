## §1 TAMP markers

Tense-Aspect-Mood-Polarity (TAMP) markers are sometimes encoded with prefixes, sometimes with free morphemes. The greeting in (1) follows a broadly parallel pattern across the languages: a temporal expression is followed by a stative marker and a predicate (in this case, an adjective).

{{cite: fieldnotes#0#d01.Road:01}}

TAMP markers are sometimes discontinuous, and go both before and after the VP (2). This, however, does not apply to all languages of Gaua, as some TAMP discontinuous markers in one language may well correspond to single TAMP markers in another.

{{cite: papers#negation_example_01}}

There is also a standalone nondumitive taking its own form (François, 2026b, p. 128), as is the case in (3).

{{cite: fieldnotes#0#d01.Road:23}}

### 1.1 The pre-verbal negator

The pre-verbal marker (NONDUM₁) occupies the position immediately before
the verbal complex. Its form shows considerable variation across the chain:
eastern Dorig retains a bisyllabic shape ({{entry: nondum1}}), while central
and western varieties have reduced to monosyllables.

The subject pronoun {{entry: 3pl}} precedes the negation complex in all
five languages, confirming its status as a free-standing grammatical word
rather than a proclitic.

### 1.2 The post-verbal negator

The post-verbal marker (NONDUM₂) follows the manner adverb
{{entry: properly}} in this example. In Koro and Olrat the marker is
identical (*wōsmele*), pointing to a shared innovation absent from the
eastern and western extremes of the chain.

The noun {{entry: speech}} heading the object noun phrase shows the
highest degree of lexical divergence in the example: no obvious
phonological correspondence links the Nume form to the Lakon form,
illustrating the rapid lexical differentiation characteristic of the
Gaua dialect chain.

### 1.3 Structural comparison

| | NONDUM₁ | NONDUM₂ |
|---|---|---|
| Nume | vitis | mi |
| Dorig | sowse | te |
| Koro | tē | wōsmele |
| Olrat | tē | wōsmele |

The partial isogloss between Koro and Olrat for both markers suggests
a common innovation at that node of the dialect network.
